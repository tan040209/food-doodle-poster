# Original Prompt (English, image-model ready)

Create a vertical creative poster from the provided real food photo: **keep the real photographed food**, then let a crowd of tiny clumsy child-drawn workers walk into the frame and tell a whimsical story. A real photo and rough children's line doodles collide, turning an ordinary snapshot of food into a fun little poster.

## Composition
- Portrait poster, aspect ~4:5 or 3:4.
- The real food is the absolute photographic anchor, ~35%-55% of the frame. Keep its photography texture, ingredient detail, gloss, sauce, sesame, fish roe, matcha powder, coconut flakes, wooden tray, ceramic ware. Do not turn it into an illustration, do not change the dish / ware / sauce / plating. You may re-cut, resize and reposition it for better composition, but it must clearly come from the original photo.
- Clean the busy background to a large warm ivory / cream / off-white field with a subtle recycled-paper grain and lots of negative space. No restaurant scene background, no gradient, no saturated color blocks.

## Micro worker doodles
- Surround the real food with many tiny primitive black-line child doodle figures: lumpy or irregular heads, dot eyes, simple smiles, stick bodies, short limbs, wrong proportions, exaggerated poses.
- Thick black hand-drawn strokes, irregular breaks, slight wobble, crayon / marker roughness, clumsy and naive. Not smooth, not vector, not kawaii sticker, not anime, not mascot.
- Workers must be clearly smaller than the food and be "processing" it like a tiny construction site / playground: climbing a ladder onto it, pushing a huge glutinous rice ball, hauling fish roe on a small cart, ladling sauce, sprinkling sesame, sweeping powder off the plate, checking food with a fork, pulling ropes, peeking over the plate edge, pushing / pulling / lifting / carrying / pouring / sprinkling / scooping / brushing, or shyly sneaking a bite. Simple, readable, funny.

## Color
- Doodles stay black. Food keeps its true colors. Only tiny food-derived accents: orange-red roe, soft green matcha, warm brown wood box, cream white rice ball. No colored clothes, no large color blocks. Overall: warm ivory, black ink, food natural colors, muted brown, soft green, small orange-red accents.

## Texture
- Photographic food + rough hand-on-paper doodles, with light paper grain. A "photo + hand-drawn intervention" contrast; do not flatten everything into illustration.

## Version A — with text
- Reserve ~20%-30% at the top for a title.
- Add a thick black, crooked, child-handwritten Chinese title: very thick strokes, uneven character proportions, crooked baselines, imperfect glyph structure, rough dry-marker texture, childlike mistakes, awkward spacing. Looks like a child wrote it with a thick marker. Not a computer font, not calligraphy, not brush script, not cute rounded, not modern design font.
- Optional short handwritten subtitle under the title; at most two text layers total.
- Suggested titles: 开烤啦 / 肉肉先上场 / 甜甜球球 / 今天加菜 / 一口一个软乎乎 / 开饭咯

## Version B — no text
- No text, letters, numbers, labels, QR codes, or gibberish anywhere. No title area. Make workers more numerous and more active; tell the full story through worker actions with the real food.

## Avoid
- No 3D cartoon, Pixar style, anime character, Q-version character, vector icon, emoji, colorful children's-book illustration, smooth digital line art, complex ad layout, neon colors, saturated background, or AI plastic look. Doodles must not cover the most important visual detail of the food.

Final impression: first you see **real, appetizing food**; only on the second look do you notice a crowd of rough little workers bustling around it.
